CCTA contrast

Measure aortic root attenuation, noise, SNR and CNR in a coronary CT angiogram, right in your browser.

This research tool is not for clinical use and has not been validated for diagnosis or treatment.

Click inside the aortic root, just above the valve, and the tool measures how bright the contrast is and how noisy the image is there. It finds the contrast-filled lumen near your click, grows a region inside it, keeps the part at least 3 mm from the wall within 12 mm of the lumen centre, and reports the median HU, the noise (a robust standard deviation) and their ratio, the SNR.

Then click fat for the contrast-to-noise ratio. Epicardial fat beside a proximal coronary artery is the usual choice, since that is the contrast a reader sees around the vessel. The tool takes the patch of fat (−190 to −30 HU) connected to your click within 5 mm, trims 1 mm off its edges, and reports its median HU. CNR is the lumen HU minus the fat HU, divided by the aortic noise.

No scan?

Measure

1. Move the crosshair into the bright aortic root, then measure the aorta.

2. Move it into fat, ideally beside a proximal coronary artery, then measure the fat.

The comparison under the aortic values places each among 158 scans from the test set of the CAS-X coronary CT dataset, measured the same way. CNR has no such comparison yet, because it depends on where the fat click goes. It is not a quality threshold, and the tool gives no pass or fail. Noise and SNR depend on the scanner, dose and reconstruction kernel, so compare scans from similar protocols.

The measurement runs on your computer, and this tool does not upload your scan. The page does load code from outside services, and the site uses Google Analytics, so please do not use it with identifiable patient data.
